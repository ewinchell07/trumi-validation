TRumi Validation Record

We took two Trossen TRumi handheld grippers from box to processed dataset, then tried them on a conveyor-belt pick-and-place at four belt speeds. The pipeline works on a static tabletop. On the conveyor, visual SLAM lost the camera pose in almost every demo, and the cause is not yet isolated.

2 grippers · bimanual GoPro HERO13 Black ×2 · Labs fw 2.10.70 2.7K 4:3 · 119.88 fps TRumi @ 8b916b9 + local patch
Session 1 · static tabletop
9episodes from 10 demos

Every clip tracked on every frame. The one deliberate off-map start was dropped, as it should be. The deliberately jerky demo was kept: nothing in the pipeline flags it.

Session 2 · conveyor, 4 belt speeds
3single-arm episodes from 20 bimanual demos

28 of 40 camera clips never produced a trajectory, and most of the rest tracked 14–32% of frames. Belt speed made no visible difference. Two clips tracked perfectly, every time we ran them.

Findings

What we learned

  1. The capture and processing chain works end to end on a static scene. Camera settings, IMU, finger-tag detection (99–100%), two-camera timecode sync (pairs start 0.03–0.15 s apart), gripper calibration (the two grippers agree to 0.6 mm) and MCAP output all check out. High confidence
  2. SLAM on the conveyor setup fails in a way belt speed does not explain. Tracking was poor at 20, 40, 60 and 80 alike. The two clips that tracked 100% were at 40 and 80. Medium: 5 demos per speed
  3. This ORB-SLAM3 build breaks when it has to re-find the map mid-clip. After relocalizing, it compares demo timestamps with the mapping video's keyframes (about −1,716 s apart), computes a velocity of about 2,500 m/s, then either flickers between tracked and lost or hangs. One finished clip relocalized 218 times in about 18 s. Standard UMI practice never exercises this path, because demos start looking at the mapped area. High: seen in the SLAM logs
  4. Starting clips on the map does not fix it (test 1). With the second map, most clips already recognized the map in their first 1–2 frames. Trimming 8 clips to start at that moment made all 8 hang, including 3 that had tracked 27–32% before. High
  5. The two perfect clips are reproducible, and we can't yet say why they differ. Camera 3778 on demos 10 and 20 tracked 100% on three separate runs. Their frames look like the failed takes (same start at the robot station, same pick, same place, same people walking around). Medium: open question
  6. Even good tabletop tracking is jumpier than Trossen's reference. In our session 1 demos, 3–12% of frame-to-frame steps jump more than 3 cm. Trossen's example has 0.2–0.6%. The jump rate does single out the fast/jerky demo (18–21%), so it could become an automatic quality filter. Medium

Next

What to do before collecting at scale

  1. Belt-off control. Record 5 demos in the same scene with the belt stopped. Good tracking would implicate the moving belt; bad tracking would implicate the scene or the SLAM build.
  2. Point at the workspace before pressing record. It avoids the mid-clip relocalization path entirely. Cheap to adopt now.
  3. Fix or bypass the relocalization bug. The −1,716 s keyframe offset points at IMU preintegration across the map/demo time gap. Resetting the IMU state on relocalization, or relocalizing visual-only, would be the first things to try in gopro_slam.cc.
  4. Find what's different about demos 10 and 20 on camera 3778. Compare per-frame feature counts and IMU traces against their failed neighbours.
  5. Add jump rate to the session report as a warning, so fast or jittery demos are flagged automatically.
